## module: Introduction to Financial Management
video: i0az1n2JXWE

### Introduction
Financial management is about acquiring funds and using them wisely. While accounting reports what happened, finance decides what to do next.

### Key concepts
- **Three major decisions:** investment, financing and dividend.
- **Goal:** maximizing shareholder wealth rather than short-term profit alone.
- **Risk and return:** higher expected return generally comes with higher risk.
- **Role of the finance manager:** planning funds, controlling them and managing risk.

### Definitions
- **Wealth maximization:** increasing the long-term value of the owners' stake.
- **Investment decision:** where to put the funds.
- **Financing decision:** how to raise the funds.
- **Dividend decision:** how much profit to distribute.

### Example
A company with ₹1 crore can choose to buy a machine (investment), raise extra money through a loan (financing), and pay part of its profit to shareholders (dividend).

### Key takeaways
- Long-term value matters more than one year's profit.
- Every financial decision balances risk and return.
- Finance depends on accounting information.

## module: Financial Planning and Forecasting
video: tYPknBUufVk

### Introduction
Financial planning sets out how much money a business will need and where it will come from. Forecasting estimates the future so that surprises are smaller.

### Key concepts
- **Sales forecast:** usually the starting point.
- **Percent-of-sales method:** projects costs and assets from sales.
- **Budgets:** turn plans into numbers.
- **Scenario analysis:** tests best, likely and worst cases.

### Definitions
- **Forecast:** an estimate of a future result.
- **Budget:** a financial plan for a period.
- **Variance:** the difference between planned and actual results.
- **External financing needed:** funds required beyond what the business generates.

### Example
Sales are expected to grow from ₹10 lakh to ₹12 lakh, and costs are about 70% of sales. Forecast costs are ₹8.4 lakh, leaving ₹3.6 lakh before other expenses.

### Key takeaways
- A forecast is always an estimate, so review it regularly.
- Compare actual results with budgets, and investigate the differences.
- Plan for bad scenarios while there is still time to act.

## module: Time Value of Money
video: -OinhNhIMG0

### Introduction
Money today is worth more than the same amount in the future, because it can earn a return. This idea underlies nearly every financial decision.

### Key concepts
- **Future value:** what money grows to.
- **Present value:** what future money is worth today.
- **Compounding:** earning interest on interest.
- **Discounting:** the reverse of compounding.
- **Annuities:** a series of equal payments.

### Definitions
- **Interest rate:** the return per period.
- **Discount rate:** the rate used to find present value.
- **Compound interest:** interest added to the principal.

### Example
```text
FV = PV x (1 + r)^n
₹10,000 at 10% for 2 years = 10,000 x 1.21 = ₹12,100
PV of ₹12,100 in 2 years at 10% = ₹10,000
```

### Key takeaways
- Only compare money amounts at the same point in time.
- Compounding grows powerful over long periods, so start early.
- A higher discount rate lowers present value.

## module: Capital Budgeting and Investment Decisions
video: NTHwQwDdQmA

### Introduction
Capital budgeting evaluates big long-term projects, such as new factories. These decisions commit large sums and are hard to reverse.

### Key concepts
- **Payback period:** how long it takes to recover the investment.
- **NPV (Net Present Value):** present value of inflows minus the cost.
- **IRR (Internal Rate of Return):** the rate at which NPV equals zero.
- **Decision rules:** accept if NPV is positive, or if IRR exceeds the required return.

### Definitions
- **Cash inflow:** money the project brings in.
- **Hurdle rate:** the minimum acceptable return.
- **Mutually exclusive projects:** choosing one rules out the others.

### Example
A project costs ₹1,00,000 and returns ₹60,000 per year for two years. At 10%:
```text
NPV = 60,000/1.1 + 60,000/1.21 - 1,00,000 = about ₹4,132
```
NPV is positive, so accept.

### Key takeaways
- NPV is generally the most reliable measure.
- Payback ignores what happens after the recovery point.
- Use cash flows, not accounting profits.

## module: Cost of Capital and Capital Structure
video: Yvi2f-AEuCM

### Introduction
Funds aren't free. The cost of capital is the return investors require, and capital structure is the mix of debt and equity used to raise it.

### Key concepts
- **Cost of debt:** interest, reduced by tax savings.
- **Cost of equity:** the return shareholders expect.
- **WACC:** the weighted average of these costs.
- **Financial leverage:** debt magnifies both profits and losses.
- **Optimal structure:** the mix that minimizes WACC.

### Definitions
- **Debt:** borrowed money with fixed obligations.
- **Equity:** owners' funds.
- **WACC:** Weighted Average Cost of Capital.
- **Tax shield:** tax saved because interest is deductible.

### Example
With 60% equity at 12%, and 40% debt costing 8% after tax:
```text
WACC = 0.6 x 12% + 0.4 x 8% = 7.2% + 3.2% = 10.4%
```
Projects should earn more than 10.4%.

### Key takeaways
- WACC is a common hurdle rate for projects.
- Debt is often cheaper but adds risk.
- Too much debt can threaten a company's survival.

## module: Working Capital Management
video: 9VSqG4CfrfM

### Introduction
Working capital is what keeps daily operations running. Even profitable companies can fail if they can't pay their short-term bills.

### Key concepts
- **Net working capital:** current assets minus current liabilities.
- **Operating cycle:** from buying stock to collecting cash.
- **Managing inventory:** enough to meet demand without tying up funds.
- **Managing receivables and payables:** collect sooner and pay on agreed terms.

### Definitions
- **Current assets:** cash, stock and receivables.
- **Current liabilities:** amounts due within a year.
- **Liquidity:** the ability to pay short-term obligations.
- **Current ratio:** current assets divided by current liabilities.

### Example
A store has ₹5 lakh of current assets and ₹2.5 lakh of current liabilities. Its current ratio is 2, and its net working capital is ₹2.5 lakh.

### Key takeaways
- Too little working capital risks being unable to pay bills.
- Too much ties up money that could be earning a return.
- Shorter operating cycles free up cash.

## module: Financial Risk Management and Analysis
video: R6QKBVwIB78

### Introduction
Businesses face uncertain interest rates, currencies and customers. Risk management identifies these exposures and decides what to do about them, while ratio analysis helps to monitor financial health.

### Key concepts
- **Market risk:** changes in prices, interest rates and exchange rates.
- **Credit risk:** customers or borrowers failing to pay.
- **Liquidity risk:** not having cash when needed.
- **Responses:** avoid, reduce, transfer or accept.
- **Ratio analysis:** liquidity, profitability and leverage measures.

### Definitions
- **Hedging:** reducing risk using an offsetting position.
- **Diversification:** spreading investments to reduce risk.
- **Derivative:** a contract whose value depends on an underlying asset.

### Example
An Indian exporter expects US dollars in three months and fears the rupee will strengthen. A forward contract fixes the exchange rate today, which removes the uncertainty.

### Key takeaways
- Not all risks can or should be removed.
- Diversification reduces risk that's specific to one investment.
- Review ratios over time and against similar companies.
