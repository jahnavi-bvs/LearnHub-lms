# course: Graphic Design Fundamentals

## module: Introduction to Graphic Design
video: dFSia1LZI4Y

### Introduction
Graphic design is visual communication: combining images, type and layout to deliver a message. Unlike pure art, it's made to solve a problem for a specific audience.

### Key concepts
- **Purpose:** to inform, persuade or guide.
- **Design fields:** branding, print, web and UI, advertising, motion.
- **Design process:** brief, research, sketch, design, feedback, refine.
- **Core tools:** Photoshop, Illustrator, InDesign, Figma and Canva.

### Definitions
- **Design brief:** a document stating a project's goals and requirements.
- **Raster image:** made of pixels, such as a photo.
- **Vector image:** made of mathematical shapes; scales without quality loss.
- **Mockup:** a realistic preview of a design.

### Example
A brief for a college fest poster states the audience (students), the message (date and venue), and the goal (increase attendance). Each design decision then serves that goal.

### Key takeaways
- Good design communicates first, and decorates second.
- Always begin with the brief and the audience.
- Use vectors for logos and raster formats for photos.

## module: Principles of Design and Composition
video: 9EPTM91TBDU

### Introduction
Design principles are guidelines for arranging elements so that a layout is clear and appealing. They explain why some designs simply work better than others.

### Key concepts
- **Balance:** visual weight spread symmetrically or asymmetrically.
- **Contrast:** differences that attract attention.
- **Hierarchy:** the order in which things get noticed.
- **Alignment, repetition, proximity:** create order and consistency.
- **Rule of thirds:** place key elements along grid lines.

### Definitions
- **Composition:** the arrangement of elements on a page.
- **White space:** the empty area around elements.
- **Focal point:** where the eye goes first.
- **Grid:** invisible lines that organize a layout.

### Example
On an event poster, a very large title (hierarchy) in high contrast against the background catches the eye. The date and venue are grouped together (proximity), and everything lines up on a grid.

### Key takeaways
- White space is a tool, not wasted space.
- If everything is emphasized, nothing stands out.
- Squint at your design to test whether the hierarchy works.

## module: Color Theory and Typography
video: _2LLXnUdUIc

### Introduction
Colour sets the mood, and typography gives words a voice. Together they strongly shape how people feel about and read a design.

### Key concepts
- **Colour wheel:** primary, secondary and tertiary colours.
- **Harmonies:** complementary, analogous and triadic.
- **Colour models:** RGB for screens and CMYK for print.
- **Type classes:** serif, sans-serif, script and display.
- **Spacing:** kerning, leading and tracking.

### Definitions
- **Hue:** the colour itself.
- **Saturation:** the colour's intensity.
- **Typeface:** a family of letter designs.
- **Leading:** the vertical space between lines of text.

### Example
A healthcare website might use calm blues with a clean sans-serif typeface to feel trustworthy. A pairing of one bold heading font and one readable body font usually works best.

### Key takeaways
- Limit designs to about two typefaces and a few colours.
- Ensure strong contrast between text and background.
- Colours carry cultural meanings, so consider your audience.

## module: Image Editing Fundamentals
video: 80434t3GTgA

### Introduction
Image editing improves photos and combines them into new designs. A few core skills cover most everyday needs.

### Key concepts
- **Layers:** separate elements stacked on top of each other.
- **Non-destructive editing:** masks and adjustment layers keep the original intact.
- **Adjustments:** brightness, contrast and colour balance.
- **Selections and masks:** isolate parts of an image.
- **File formats:** JPEG, PNG, SVG and PSD.

### Definitions
- **Resolution:** pixels per inch; 300 PPI is typical for print, and screens need less.
- **Layer mask:** hides parts of a layer without deleting them.
- **Crop:** trim the edges of an image.

### Example
To put a person on a new background: select the person, add a layer mask, place the background on a lower layer, and match the colours. Because a mask is used, you can still refine the edges later.

### Key takeaways
- Keep an editable original and work on copies.
- Use PNG for transparency and JPEG for photos.
- Only use images you have the right to use.

## module: Logo and Branding Design
video: l9_BM1opTj8

### Introduction
A logo identifies a company, and a brand is the full impression people have of it. Strong branding stays consistent everywhere it appears.

### Key concepts
- **Logo types:** wordmark, lettermark, symbol and combination.
- **Qualities:** simple, memorable, scalable and timeless.
- **Brand identity:** logo, colours, typography and tone.
- **Style guide:** rules for using a brand consistently.

### Definitions
- **Wordmark:** a logo made of the name in stylized type.
- **Brand identity:** the visual system representing a brand.
- **Style guide:** a document that defines brand rules.

### Example
Before designing a logo for a learning platform, research its audience and competitors. Sketch many ideas, refine the best in black and white first, then add colour, and test it at small sizes.

### Key takeaways
- Design logos as vectors, so they scale to any size.
- A good logo works in a single colour.
- Check it's readable at small sizes, such as a browser icon.

## module: Social Media and Digital Design
video: LEEiSUlfgIo

### Introduction
Designing for social media means capturing attention quickly on small screens. Each platform has its own sizes, habits and expectations.

### Key concepts
- **Platform formats:** square, vertical and landscape sizes.
- **Scroll-stopping layouts:** bold visuals with very little text.
- **Templates:** keep a series of posts consistent.
- **Mobile-first:** check all text at phone size.
- **Accessibility:** use readable text and good contrast.

### Definitions
- **Aspect ratio:** width compared with height, such as 1:1 or 9:16.
- **Call to action:** a prompt such as "Enroll now".
- **Safe zone:** an area free from interface overlays.

### Example
For a course launch post, use a 1:1 image with one strong headline, the course name, a single call to action, and brand colours. Save the details for the caption rather than the image.

### Key takeaways
- People scroll fast, so make one clear point per post.
- Use templates to stay consistent and work faster.
- Keep important content inside the safe zones.

## module: Practical Graphic Design Projects
video: f0nS9JIPurQ

### Introduction
Skill grows through making things. Practical projects apply everything you've learned, and they also build a portfolio to show employers or clients.

### Key concepts
- **Project ideas:** poster, logo, magazine spread, social media set, packaging.
- **Working from a brief:** practise with real or invented clients.
- **Feedback:** critique shows you what you can't see yourself.
- **Portfolio:** show your process as well as the final work.

### Definitions
- **Portfolio:** a selection of your best work.
- **Critique:** a structured review of a design.
- **Case study:** an explanation of a project's problem, process and result.

### Example
Create a complete identity for a fictional café: logo, menu, a poster and three social media posts. Then write a short case study explaining your design choices.

### Key takeaways
- Quality matters more than quantity in a portfolio.
- Seek critique early, and revise your work.
- Recreating good designs is useful practice, but never present them as your own.
