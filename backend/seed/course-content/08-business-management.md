# course: Principles of Business Management

## module: Introduction to Business and Management
video: a-uPznyA7Hw

### Introduction
A business provides goods or services, usually for profit. Management is the work of getting people and resources to achieve its goals effectively.

### Key concepts
- **Business types:** sole proprietorship, partnership and company.
- **Stakeholders:** owners, employees, customers, suppliers and society.
- **Levels of management:** top, middle and first-line.
- **Management skills:** technical, human and conceptual.

### Definitions
- **Management:** achieving goals through people and resources.
- **Efficiency:** doing things right, with minimal waste.
- **Effectiveness:** doing the right things, achieving the goal.
- **Stakeholder:** anyone affected by the business.

### Example
In a bakery chain, the CEO decides whether to expand (top level), a regional manager plans store targets (middle level), and a shift supervisor organizes today's baking staff (first-line).

### Key takeaways
- Good managers need both efficiency and effectiveness.
- Higher levels need more conceptual skill; lower levels need more technical skill.
- A business must balance the interests of all stakeholders.

## module: Principles and Functions of Management
video: WC6u9EskeN8

### Introduction
Management work follows recognizable patterns. Classical thinkers such as Henri Fayol described functions and principles that remain widely used today.

### Key concepts
- **Four functions:** planning, organizing, leading and controlling.
- **Fayol's principles:** include division of work, authority and responsibility, unity of command, and unity of direction.
- **Scientific management:** Frederick Taylor's study of work to improve efficiency.
- **The cycle:** controlling results feeds back into new plans.

### Definitions
- **Planning:** deciding goals and how to reach them.
- **Organizing:** arranging people and resources.
- **Leading:** motivating and directing people.
- **Controlling:** comparing results with goals and correcting them.

### Example
Launching a product: set a sales target (planning), form a team with clear roles (organizing), motivate them (leading), and check monthly sales against the target to adjust (controlling).

### Key takeaways
- The four functions form a continuous cycle.
- Unity of command means each person reports to one manager.
- Principles are guidelines to adapt, not rigid rules.

## module: Planning and Decision Making
video: S0vKNan8YTs

### Introduction
Planning sets direction, and decision making chooses among options. Sound planning reduces uncertainty and focuses effort.

### Key concepts
- **Types of plans:** strategic (long-term), tactical (medium-term), operational (short-term).
- **SMART goals:** Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound.
- **Decision process:** identify the problem, list options, evaluate, choose, implement, review.
- **SWOT analysis:** Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats.

### Definitions
- **Objective:** a specific result to achieve.
- **Strategy:** the overall approach to reaching objectives.
- **Programmed decision:** a routine choice with a set rule.
- **Non-programmed decision:** a new, complex choice.

### Example
"Improve sales" is a vague goal. "Increase online sales by 15% by December through a new mobile site" is SMART, because it's specific, measurable and has a deadline.

### Key takeaways
- A goal you can't measure is hard to manage.
- Plans should be reviewed as conditions change.
- Big decisions benefit from gathering data and other viewpoints.

## module: Organizing and Staffing
video: 5kh5VRG6Zfk

### Introduction
Organizing divides work and defines who reports to whom. Staffing makes sure the right people fill those roles.

### Key concepts
- **Organizational structures:** functional, divisional and matrix.
- **Delegation:** assigning work along with the authority to do it.
- **Span of control:** the number of people a manager supervises.
- **Staffing process:** planning, recruitment, selection, training, appraisal.

### Definitions
- **Authority:** the right to make decisions.
- **Responsibility:** the duty to complete a task.
- **Accountability:** answering for results.
- **Recruitment:** attracting suitable candidates.

### Example
A growing company organized by function has marketing and finance departments. As it enters new countries, it may shift to a divisional structure, with one division for each region.

### Key takeaways
- Authority and responsibility should match.
- Delegation develops staff and frees managers for bigger issues.
- Hiring and training well are investments, not just costs.

## module: Leadership and Motivation
video: qzoIAJYPQwo

### Introduction
Managers run processes, while leaders inspire people. Understanding what motivates people helps teams perform willingly rather than under pressure.

### Key concepts
- **Leadership styles:** autocratic, democratic and laissez-faire.
- **Maslow's hierarchy:** physiological, safety, social, esteem, self-actualization.
- **Herzberg's theory:** hygiene factors prevent dissatisfaction; motivators create satisfaction.
- **Theory X and Y:** McGregor's contrasting assumptions about workers.

### Definitions
- **Leadership:** influencing people toward a goal.
- **Motivation:** the drive that directs effort.
- **Intrinsic motivation:** enjoying the work itself.
- **Extrinsic motivation:** external rewards, such as pay.

### Example
Raising pay may stop complaints, but it may not inspire better work (a Herzberg hygiene factor). Giving recognition and more challenging projects is more likely to motivate.

### Key takeaways
- No single leadership style suits every situation.
- People are motivated by different things, so get to know your team.
- Recognition is powerful and costs little.

## module: Communication and Team Management
video: 4BO42rTCZS8

### Introduction
Most management problems involve communication. Strong teams depend on clear information, shared goals and trust.

### Key concepts
- **Communication process:** sender, message, channel, receiver, feedback.
- **Barriers:** noise, jargon, assumptions and emotions.
- **Tuckman's team stages:** forming, storming, norming, performing, adjourning.
- **Conflict:** handled well, it can improve decisions.

### Definitions
- **Active listening:** focusing on understanding rather than preparing a reply.
- **Feedback:** information about performance.
- **Team cohesion:** how strongly members work together.

### Example
A manager says "get the report done soon", and the team assumes next week. "Please send the sales report by 5 pm Friday" avoids that confusion by making the deadline clear.

### Key takeaways
- Check that your message was understood.
- Give feedback that is specific, timely and about behaviour.
- Expect early conflict in new teams; it's a normal stage.

## module: Business Strategy and Organizational Growth
video: JVffzkcI02M

### Introduction
Strategy is how a business competes and grows over the long term. It decides what to do, and just as importantly, what not to do.

### Key concepts
- **Porter's generic strategies:** cost leadership, differentiation and focus.
- **Porter's Five Forces:** analyses competition in an industry.
- **Competitive advantage:** what you do better than rivals.
- **Growth options:** new markets, new products, mergers and acquisitions.
- **Ansoff Matrix:** maps growth by products and markets.

### Definitions
- **Cost leadership:** competing by having the lowest costs.
- **Differentiation:** offering something uniquely valued.
- **Market share:** your portion of total sales in a market.

### Example
A budget airline competes on cost, with low fares and basic service. A premium airline differentiates on comfort. A company trying to do both risks being "stuck in the middle".

### Key takeaways
- A strategy means making clear choices.
- Growth should build on real strengths.
- Review strategy as competitors and markets change.
