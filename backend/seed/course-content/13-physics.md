# course: Fundamentals of Physics

## module: Introduction to Physics and Measurement
video: 2I8XMxMvWl0

### Introduction
Physics studies matter, energy and how they interact. Because it relies on experiments, accurate measurement is its foundation.

### Key concepts
- **SI base units:** metre, kilogram, second, ampere, kelvin, mole, candela.
- **Derived units:** such as newton and joule, built from base units.
- **Scalars and vectors:** size alone, or size and direction.
- **Significant figures:** show how precise a measurement is.

### Definitions
- **Physical quantity:** something that can be measured.
- **Accuracy:** closeness to the true value.
- **Precision:** how close repeated measurements are to each other.
- **Dimensional analysis:** checking equations using their units.

### Example
Speed is measured in metres per second. To convert 72 km/h:
```text
72 km/h = 72 x 1000 m / 3600 s = 20 m/s
```

### Key takeaways
- Always include units with a value.
- Quantities can be added only if they have the same units.
- A measurement can be precise without being accurate.

## module: Motion and Laws of Motion
video: g550H4e5FCY

### Introduction
Kinematics describes how objects move, and Newton's laws explain why. Together they can predict the motion of nearly everything in daily life.

### Key concepts
- **First law:** an object keeps its motion unless a net force acts (inertia).
- **Second law:** F = ma.
- **Third law:** every action has an equal and opposite reaction.
- **Equations of motion:** connect velocity, acceleration, time and distance.

### Definitions
- **Velocity:** speed in a given direction.
- **Acceleration:** the rate of change of velocity.
- **Force:** a push or pull, measured in newtons.
- **Inertia:** resistance to a change in motion.

### Example
A 1000 kg car accelerates at 2 m/s².
```text
F = m x a = 1000 x 2 = 2000 N
```
A car starting at rest reaches v = at = 2 x 5 = 10 m/s after 5 seconds.

### Key takeaways
- Motion doesn't need a force; a change in motion does.
- Action and reaction forces act on different objects.
- Mass isn't weight: weight is the force of gravity on a mass.

## module: Work, Energy and Power
video: w4QFJb9a8vo

### Introduction
Energy lets things happen, and it transforms between forms but is never destroyed. Work transfers energy, and power tells how fast that happens.

### Key concepts
- **Work:** W = F d cos(theta).
- **Kinetic energy:** KE = 1/2 m v².
- **Potential energy:** PE = m g h.
- **Conservation of energy:** the total energy stays constant.
- **Power:** P = W / t.

### Definitions
- **Joule:** the unit of work and energy.
- **Watt:** the unit of power, one joule per second.
- **Efficiency:** useful energy out divided by energy in.

### Example
A 2 kg ball falls from 5 m, ignoring air resistance (g = 10 m/s²):
```text
PE = 2 x 10 x 5 = 100 J
At the ground: 1/2 x 2 x v² = 100, so v = 10 m/s
```

### Key takeaways
- No work is done if there's no movement along the force.
- Energy changes form, often into heat.
- Doubling speed makes kinetic energy four times larger.

## module: Gravitation
video: HjKL7R-aTJE

### Introduction
Gravity pulls every mass toward every other mass. The same force that makes an apple fall also keeps the planets in orbit.

### Key concepts
- **Newton's law of gravitation:** F = G m1 m2 / r².
- **Inverse square law:** doubling the distance makes the force four times weaker.
- **Gravitational field:** the force per unit mass around an object.
- **Orbits:** gravity provides the required centripetal force.

### Definitions
- **G:** the gravitational constant, 6.67 x 10⁻¹¹ N m²/kg².
- **g:** acceleration due to gravity, about 9.8 m/s² on Earth.
- **Weight:** W = m g.
- **Escape velocity:** the speed required to leave a body's gravity.

### Example
A 60 kg person has a weight of about 588 N on Earth. On the Moon, where g is about 1.6 m/s², they weigh about 96 N, but their mass is still 60 kg.

### Key takeaways
- Mass doesn't change between planets, but weight does.
- Without air resistance, all objects fall at the same rate.
- Astronauts in orbit are in continuous free fall, which is why they float.

## module: Properties of Matter and Fluids
video: kkq8ruV8_Jw

### Introduction
Materials respond to forces in different ways, and liquids and gases flow. These properties explain why ships float and how hydraulic brakes work.

### Key concepts
- **Density:** mass per unit volume.
- **Pressure:** P = F / A.
- **Pascal's principle:** pressure on a confined fluid spreads equally.
- **Archimedes' principle:** buoyant force equals the weight of fluid displaced.
- **Bernoulli's principle:** faster-moving fluid has lower pressure.

### Definitions
- **Elasticity:** returning to shape after a force is removed.
- **Viscosity:** a fluid's resistance to flow.
- **Buoyancy:** the upward force from a fluid.
- **Surface tension:** the tendency of a liquid's surface to resist force.

### Example
Pressure in a liquid increases with depth:
```text
P = density x g x depth
At 10 m in water: 1000 x 10 x 10 = 100,000 Pa
```

### Key takeaways
- Sharp objects cut well because small area means high pressure.
- An object floats if it's less dense overall than the fluid.
- Hydraulic systems multiply force using Pascal's principle.

## module: Heat and Thermodynamics
video: LMVIVwD6s2s

### Introduction
Thermodynamics studies heat, temperature and energy transfer. It explains engines and refrigerators, and why some processes only happen in one direction.

### Key concepts
- **Temperature and heat:** temperature measures particle motion; heat is energy in transfer.
- **Heat transfer:** conduction, convection and radiation.
- **First law:** energy is conserved.
- **Second law:** heat flows naturally from hot to cold, and entropy tends to increase.

### Definitions
- **Specific heat capacity:** the energy needed to warm 1 kg by 1 °C.
- **Latent heat:** energy absorbed or released in a change of state.
- **Entropy:** a measure of disorder.

### Example
Heating 2 kg of water by 10 °C (specific heat is about 4200 J/kg °C):
```text
Q = m c delta-T = 2 x 4200 x 10 = 84,000 J
```

### Key takeaways
- Heat and temperature are different things.
- No heat engine can be 100% efficient.
- Water's high specific heat helps to stabilize climates.

## module: Waves and Sound
video: Y1LbQBgvISs

### Introduction
Waves carry energy from one place to another without carrying matter along with them. Sound is a mechanical wave that needs a medium to travel through.

### Key concepts
- **Transverse waves:** vibrations perpendicular to travel.
- **Longitudinal waves:** vibrations parallel to travel, such as sound.
- **Wave equation:** v = f x wavelength.
- **Sound properties:** pitch depends on frequency, and loudness on amplitude.
- **Doppler effect:** pitch changes as a source moves.

### Definitions
- **Frequency:** waves per second, measured in hertz.
- **Wavelength:** the distance between wave peaks.
- **Amplitude:** the maximum displacement.
- **Echo:** a reflected sound.

### Example
Sound travels at about 340 m/s in air. A 170 Hz tone has:
```text
wavelength = v / f = 340 / 170 = 2 m
```

### Key takeaways
- Sound can't travel through a vacuum.
- Sound travels faster in solids than in air.
- A passing ambulance's pitch drops because of the Doppler effect.

## module: Electricity and Magnetism
video: s94suB5uLWw

### Introduction
Electricity and magnetism are two aspects of one force. Their connection makes motors, generators and the power grid possible.

### Key concepts
- **Ohm's law:** V = I R.
- **Circuits:** series and parallel.
- **Electric power:** P = V I.
- **Magnetic fields:** produced by moving charges.
- **Electromagnetic induction:** a changing magnetic field creates a current.

### Definitions
- **Current:** the flow of charge, measured in amperes.
- **Voltage:** the energy per charge, measured in volts.
- **Resistance:** opposition to current, measured in ohms.
- **Transformer:** changes the voltage of alternating current.

### Example
A 12 V battery connected to a 4 ohm resistor:
```text
I = V / R = 12 / 4 = 3 A
P = V x I = 12 x 3 = 36 W
```

### Key takeaways
- In series, components share voltage; in parallel, they share current.
- Motors turn electricity into motion; generators do the reverse.
- Household appliances are wired in parallel, so each gets full voltage.
