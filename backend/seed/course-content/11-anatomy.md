# course: Human Anatomy & Physiology

## module: Introduction to Human Anatomy and Physiology
video: uBGl2BujkPQ

### Introduction
Anatomy studies the body's structures, and physiology studies how they work. The two are closely linked, because a structure's form relates to its function.

### Key concepts
- **Levels of organization:** chemical, cellular, tissue, organ, organ system, organism.
- **Homeostasis:** keeping internal conditions stable.
- **Anatomical position:** the standard reference posture.
- **Directional terms:** superior, inferior, anterior, posterior, medial and lateral.

### Definitions
- **Anatomy:** the study of body structure.
- **Physiology:** the study of body function.
- **Homeostasis:** maintaining a stable internal environment.
- **Negative feedback:** a response that reverses a change.

### Example
When body temperature rises, sensors signal the brain, which triggers sweating and widens skin blood vessels. Cooling then brings the temperature back down. This is negative feedback.

### Key takeaways
- Structure and function always go together.
- Most physiological control works by negative feedback.
- Learning directional terms makes all later anatomy easier.

## module: Cells, Tissues and Body Organization
video: MB6mE6weCS4

### Introduction
The cell is the basic unit of life. Similar cells form tissues, tissues form organs, and organs work together in systems.

### Key concepts
- **Cell parts:** membrane, nucleus, mitochondria, ribosomes.
- **Four tissue types:** epithelial, connective, muscle and nervous.
- **Organs:** contain several tissue types working together.
- **Body cavities:** spaces that protect internal organs.

### Definitions
- **Tissue:** a group of similar cells with a shared function.
- **Organ:** a structure made of different tissues.
- **Organelle:** a specialized structure within a cell.
- **Mitochondria:** produce most of the cell's energy (ATP).

### Example
The stomach is an organ: epithelial tissue lines it and secretes digestive juices, muscle tissue churns food, connective tissue holds it together, and nervous tissue controls its activity.

### Key takeaways
- Each level builds on the one below it.
- All four tissue types appear in most organs.
- A cell's shape reflects its job.

## module: Skeletal and Muscular Systems
video: 656ZfzFD-ec

### Introduction
Bones and muscles work together to support the body and allow movement. The adult human skeleton has 206 bones and hundreds of skeletal muscles.

### Key concepts
- **Skeleton functions:** support, protection, movement, mineral storage, blood cell production.
- **Divisions:** axial (skull, spine, ribs) and appendicular (limbs, girdles).
- **Joints:** where bones meet.
- **Muscle types:** skeletal, cardiac and smooth.
- **Antagonistic pairs:** muscles pull but cannot push.

### Definitions
- **Tendon:** connects muscle to bone.
- **Ligament:** connects bone to bone.
- **Cartilage:** flexible cushioning tissue.
- **Sarcomere:** the basic contracting unit of a muscle.

### Example
Bending your elbow: the biceps contracts while the triceps relaxes. Straightening it reverses this. Because muscles only pull, movement requires these opposing pairs.

### Key takeaways
- Red bone marrow makes blood cells.
- Skeletal muscle is voluntary; cardiac and smooth muscle are involuntary.
- Weight-bearing exercise helps to strengthen bones.

## module: Nervous System and Sense Organs
video: RNLceVI8jcc

### Introduction
The nervous system is the body's fast communication network. It senses changes, processes information and directs responses.

### Key concepts
- **Central nervous system:** the brain and spinal cord.
- **Peripheral nervous system:** nerves throughout the body.
- **Neurons:** carry electrical signals.
- **Synapses:** chemical signalling between neurons.
- **Senses:** sight, hearing, taste, smell and touch.

### Definitions
- **Neuron:** a nerve cell.
- **Neurotransmitter:** a chemical that crosses a synapse.
- **Reflex:** a fast automatic response.
- **Autonomic nervous system:** controls involuntary functions such as heart rate.

### Example
If you touch something hot, sensory neurons signal the spinal cord, which immediately activates motor neurons to pull your hand away. You feel the pain only after the reflex has begun.

### Key takeaways
- Signals are electrical along a neuron and chemical across a synapse.
- Reflexes protect you by bypassing the brain's decision making.
- Sympathetic means "fight or flight"; parasympathetic means "rest and digest".

## module: Cardiovascular and Respiratory Systems
video: 9fxm85Fy4sQ

### Introduction
These two systems work as a team. The lungs bring in oxygen and remove carbon dioxide, while the heart and blood vessels carry those gases around the body.

### Key concepts
- **Heart:** four chambers with two atria and two ventricles.
- **Double circulation:** pulmonary (to the lungs) and systemic (to the body).
- **Blood vessels:** arteries, veins and capillaries.
- **Gas exchange:** occurs in the alveoli.
- **Breathing:** driven by the diaphragm.

### Definitions
- **Artery:** carries blood away from the heart.
- **Vein:** carries blood toward the heart.
- **Alveoli:** tiny air sacs in the lungs.
- **Haemoglobin:** the oxygen-carrying protein in red blood cells.

### Example
Blood returns from the body to the right heart, goes to the lungs to pick up oxygen, returns to the left heart, and is then pumped through the aorta to the body.

### Key takeaways
- Valves stop blood from flowing backward.
- Exchange with tissues happens through thin capillary walls.
- The pulmonary artery is unusual: it carries oxygen-poor blood.

## module: Digestive System and Nutrition
video: yIoTRGfcMqM

### Introduction
The digestive system breaks food down into molecules that the body can absorb and use. Nutrition is about which of those molecules the body needs.

### Key concepts
- **Path of food:** mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine.
- **Accessory organs:** liver, gallbladder and pancreas.
- **Enzymes:** chemically break food down.
- **Absorption:** most occurs in the small intestine.
- **Macronutrients:** carbohydrates, proteins and fats.

### Definitions
- **Peristalsis:** muscle waves that move food along.
- **Enzyme:** a protein that speeds up a reaction.
- **Villi:** finger-like folds that increase intestinal surface area.
- **Bile:** a fluid from the liver that helps digest fats.

### Example
Starch begins breaking down in the mouth through salivary amylase. Protein digestion starts in the acidic stomach. Most digestion finishes in the small intestine, where nutrients enter the blood.

### Key takeaways
- The small intestine does most of the absorption.
- The large intestine mainly absorbs water.
- A balanced diet includes macronutrients, vitamins, minerals and fibre.

## module: Excretory and Urinary Systems
video: q5qaGHfdmYM

### Introduction
Metabolism produces waste that must be removed. The urinary system filters blood and also controls the body's water and salt balance.

### Key concepts
- **Urinary organs:** kidneys, ureters, bladder and urethra.
- **Nephron:** the kidney's functional unit.
- **Urine formation:** filtration, reabsorption and secretion.
- **Other excretory organs:** lungs, skin and liver.

### Definitions
- **Nephron:** a tiny filtering unit in the kidney.
- **Urea:** a nitrogen waste product made in the liver.
- **Glomerulus:** the capillary network where filtration starts.
- **ADH:** a hormone that helps the kidneys retain water.

### Example
When you are dehydrated, ADH is released and the kidneys reabsorb more water. Your urine becomes darker and more concentrated, which conserves water in the body.

### Key takeaways
- Each kidney contains about a million nephrons.
- Most filtered water is reabsorbed back into the blood.
- Kidneys also help control blood pressure.

## module: Endocrine and Reproductive Systems
video: eWHH9je2zG4

### Introduction
The endocrine system controls the body using chemical messengers carried in the blood. Its hormones regulate growth, metabolism and reproduction.

### Key concepts
- **Major glands:** pituitary, thyroid, adrenal and pancreas.
- **Hormone action:** affects only cells with matching receptors.
- **Feedback loops:** keep hormone levels in range.
- **Reproductive organs:** testes and ovaries produce sex cells and hormones.

### Definitions
- **Hormone:** a chemical messenger carried in the blood.
- **Gland:** an organ that secretes substances.
- **Insulin:** lowers blood sugar.
- **Gonads:** the testes and ovaries.

### Example
After a meal, blood sugar rises and the pancreas releases insulin, which helps cells take in glucose. Between meals, glucagon signals the liver to release stored glucose.

### Key takeaways
- Nerve signals are fast and brief; hormones are slower and last longer.
- The pituitary is often called the "master gland".
- Hormones such as estrogen and testosterone drive reproductive development.
